# E.ON SE - Full Research

## Company Overview

- **Founded:** 2000 (from VEBA/VIAG merger), 26 years old
- **HQ:** Essen, Germany (DAX-listed as EOAN)
- **Employees:** ~78,500 globally
- **Industry:** Energy utility - Europe's largest energy network operator, 50M+ customers across 30+ countries
- **Market cap:** ~€50B (March 2026)

## Financials / Profitability

- **Revenue FY2024:** €81.7B
- **Net income FY2024:** €4.53B (very profitable)
- **Net income FY2025:** €1.73B (down 62% YoY - driven by write-downs/one-offs, not operational decline)
- **Adjusted EBITDA FY2025:** €9.8B (up 9% YoY - underlying operations are strong)
- **Investment plan:** €48B through 2030 for infrastructure modernization
- **Verdict:** Solidly profitable, massive cash flows. **Passes.**

## German GmbH & Engineering Entity

- **E.ON Digital Technology GmbH** - registered at HRB 57814 (Hannover), with branch offices including **Arnulfstr. 203, 80634 München**
- ~3,500 employees across 11 countries, dedicated to E.ON's digital transformation
- This is a genuine engineering entity - not sales/marketing. It employs software engineers, data engineers, AI engineers, and platform engineers directly
- Additional German entities: E.ON Energie Deutschland GmbH (Munich, HRB 209327), E.ON SE (Essen)
- **Verdict: Passes.** German GmbH with direct engineering contracts confirmed.

## Munich Office

## Stability & Layoffs

- No significant recent layoffs found in E.ON Digital Technology specifically
- The referenced "11,000 job cuts" article is from ~2014-2016 (nuclear phase-out restructuring) - historical, not current
- 200 staff cuts in Sweden (Dec 2024) in E.ON's energy business - small scale, not tech
- The €48B investment plan through 2030 signals growth, not contraction

- **Verdict: Strong pass.** Very stable employer, essential infrastructure business.

## Regulated Industry Caution

Criteria flag regulated industry as a concern. E.ON is heavily regulated (energy networks, grid operations, customer pricing). Practical implications:
- More bureaucracy, slower decision-making compared to pure tech companies
- Compliance requirements can constrain engineering agility
- However, E.ON Digital Technology was specifically carved out to operate more like a tech company within the E.ON structure
- Glassdoor's 4.2 WLB and 89% software dev recommendation rate suggest the tech division is somewhat insulated from the worst of utility bureaucracy

## Appendix: Toxic Leadership Assessment

**Short answer: No clear signal of toxic leadership, but there are typical large-utility bureaucracy complaints.**

### What the reviews actually say

**The worst review** comes from **E.ON Inhouse Consulting (ECON)** - described as "riddled with dishonesty, hierarchy, bureaucracy and very toxic personalities with big egos." That's a consulting division, not the Digital Technology engineering arm. The same review flags a forced-ranking performance system and managers who are "egoistic and obsessed with their positions."

**Germany-wide Glassdoor themes (cons):**
- "Overkill on processes and poor management"
- "No chance of progression unless you're related to one of the managers"
- Hierarchical structure where seniority/tenure matters more than merit
- Strategy that shifts based on "what senior management want rather than customer or market need"
- Sugar-coating from management, unclear career paths

**Sweden (Malmö):** Some reports of backstabbing, blame-shifting, and lack of accountability - but this is E.ON's energy operations, not the tech division.

### Mitigating factors

- The **Software Development division** specifically scores 4.1/5 with **89% recommend** - notably higher than the company average (76%). This suggests the tech arm is culturally distinct from the broader utility bureaucracy.
- Germany-specific WLB is 4.2/5 and 86% recommend - both strong
- No C-suite instability or high-profile leadership scandals found
- No pattern of mass layoffs in the tech division

### Verdict

The toxic signals are concentrated in **non-tech divisions** (consulting, energy operations in Scandinavia). The Digital Technology arm appears to be a healthier pocket within the broader E.ON structure. The real risk isn't toxicity - it's **corporate bureaucracy and slow pace**, which is typical for a 78,000-person utility. If past corporate experience was manageable, E.ON Digital Technology would likely feel similar.

This is not a toxic leadership concern. It's a "large German corporation with hierarchy and process overhead" concern.
