# Adobe - Employer Research Report


*Research date: 2026-05-26*

---

## Company Overview

Adobe Inc. (NASDAQ: ADBE) is an American software company founded in 1982, headquartered in San Jose, California. It is one of the largest software companies in the world, with three main product lines: Creative Cloud (Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere, After Effects, etc.), Document Cloud (Acrobat, PDF services), and Digital Experience (marketing analytics, commerce). Adobe employs approximately 30,000 people globally. It has been public for decades and is a mature, highly profitable SaaS company.

### Munich Office

The registered Munich office is at Georg-Brauchle-Ring 56/58, 80992 München (Moosach district). The U-Bahn station Georg-Brauchle-Ring is served by U1/U7 and U2.

**However**: search results and job postings suggest the Munich office is primarily a **sales and enterprise account office**, not an engineering hub. Engineering roles (Cloud Software Engineer, Senior Computer Scientist) appear concentrated at Adobe's **Hamburg** office, 780 km away. This is a significant qualification - see Role Fit section.

### Profitability and Stability - ✅ EXCELLENT

Adobe is one of the most profitable software companies in the world:

| Metric | FY2025 |
|--------|--------|
| Revenue | $23.8 billion (+11% YoY) |
| Net income | $7.13 billion (+28% YoY) |
| Net profit margin | 30% |
| Diluted EPS | $16.73 (vs $12.44 prior year) |

FY2026 guidance targets double-digit ARR growth. This is definitively not a VC-subsidized startup - Adobe has been profitable for decades and will not run out of runway.

**Notable instability signals (2025-2026):**
- CEO transition: Shantanu Narayen (38-year Adobe veteran) announced his departure in March 2026. Stock is down approximately 25% year-to-date as of early 2026 amid investor uncertainty.
- "Silent layoffs" via performance-improvement-plan (PIP) waves reported on Blind throughout late 2025 and into Q1/Q2 2026, primarily affecting Marketo, Workfront (acquired products), Digital Experience product teams, and the Noida engineering office. No formal mass-layoff announcement filed.
- Failed $20B Figma acquisition (2022-2023): EU and UK regulators blocked it on antitrust grounds; Adobe paid a $1 billion termination fee. Figma is now a stronger, independent competitor.

These are real headwinds but do not threaten Adobe's fundamental financial solidity. The concern is more about strategic drift and morale than survival.

### Work Environment - ✅ English-speaking, Hybrid

Adobe is a US-headquartered company. English is the primary working language across all technical and product teams globally. German-language job descriptions do not appear to be the norm for engineering roles.

Work policy: Adobe operates a **hybrid model (approximately 50% in-office)**, consistent for 3+ years. Some roles are more flexible depending on team and management. This is manageable and consistent with market norms.

### Role Fit

**Engineering in Munich vs. Hamburg:**
Adobe's software engineering in Germany (Cloud Platform, Senior Computer Scientist roles) appears concentrated in **Hamburg**, not Munich. The Munich office at Georg-Brauchle-Ring is primarily a **sales, account management, and enterprise consulting office**. LinkedIn lists 150-591 Munich jobs (numbers vary), but many of these are sales-adjacent roles (Account Executive, Sales Engineer, Consulting roles for Digital Experience products) rather than backend/platform engineering.

**AI/ML engineering:**
Adobe's primary AI engineering work (Firefly generative AI models, AI platform for Creative Cloud) is based in **San Jose, CA** and US offices. The 2026 AI/ML engineering internship and full-time postings are all US-based. There is no evidence of an ML engineering or AI platform team operating out of Germany.

**Tech stack:**
Where engineering roles exist in Germany (Hamburg Cloud Platform), the stack includes distributed systems and cloud infrastructure work - potentially Python-adjacent but less clear. ML/AI engineering in Munich specifically is not evidenced.

**Summary of role fit concern:** Even though Adobe checks nearly all structural criteria (GmbH, ratings, salary, stability, product company). Current evidence suggests the Munich office does not have this engineering function. Hamburg has some, but is a different city.

Adobe is unambiguously a product company with three major owned product lines generating $23.8B in annual revenue. It is not a consulting company. The Digital Experience division has some professional-services adjacent roles, but core engineering is product-focused.

## Interview Process

For reference when applying:

1. **Recruiter screen** (~30-45 min): background, interest in Adobe AI products (Firefly), location/comp fit
2. **Hiring Manager screen**: deep dive on past projects, ownership, and technical trade-offs
3. **Online assessment**: HackerRank - 2 medium-difficulty DSA problems + 10-15 MCQs (OS, DBMS, networking)
4. **Technical rounds** (2-3): coding (LeetCode Medium level, clean production-quality code emphasis), system design/architecture, debugging exercise
5. **Behavioral/values round**: tied to Adobe's "Create the Future" (AI awareness, innovation) and "Own the Outcome" (accountability, initiative) values - this is reportedly a significant filter

Total process: 3-6 weeks. Response time after final loop: 10-15 business days.

Adobe passes nearly every structural criterion comfortably - the GmbH is real, the ratings are clean, the compensation is generous, and it is definitively a stable, profitable product company. The work-life balance reviews from Germany are positive and there are no toxic culture red flags.

Before targeting Adobe seriously, the first step should be to search active vacancies on Adobe's careers portal and LinkedIn filtered to Munich + Software Engineer / Engineering - and verify that these are backend/platform/AI roles rather than sales engineering or consulting. If the only engineering in Germany is in Hamburg, Adobe is off the list without a relocation.

The CEO transition and silent-layoff pattern are worth monitoring but are not disqualifying for a German-office hire given the financial solidity of the business.
