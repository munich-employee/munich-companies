### Business Model

B2G defence software. Appia is a logistics decision-support and command system for logistics levels 1 and 2 (brigade/battalion), sitting between ERP (SASPF, the Bundeswehr SAP system), NATO LOGFAS and C2/C4I systems. It builds a Recognized Logistic Picture (RLP), forecasts demand from stock data, optimises multimodal resupply routes, runs offline on a field server in the brigade command post, and is operated via hardened tablets/laptops. Go-to-market is direct to armed forces plus partnership with Thales (Appia integrated into HexaForce, announced 23 Jun 2026). Sales cycles in defence procurement are long, which is the main reason early revenue is unlikely.

## Section 9: Regulated Industry

Defence. Practical impact for engineers:
- **Security clearance:** work on Bundeswehr systems and data, especially on-site with units, typically requires a Sicherheitsüberprüfung (SÜ1/SÜ2 under SÜG) and handling of classified material (VS-NfD and above), with process overhead and background checks lasting months.
- **Export control:** EU Dual-Use Regulation and German AWG/AWV; Project A describes the product as "built for dual-use deployment".
- **Accreditation and certification** for deployment on military networks (BSI/Bundeswehr IT security accreditation, NATO interoperability standards such as JLSN), with documentation overhead.
- **On-prem, air-gapped delivery:** releases ship to customer hardware rather than continuous deployment; slower feedback loops and field testing during exercises.
- Ethical dimension: the product is logistics/decision support, not weapons, but it is directly part of military operations (ammunition, fuel, spare parts resupply).

**Verdict: CAUTION**

## Section 13: Verdict

**Exclude for now; revisit only if funding, headcount and a Munich presence are confirmed in writing.**

**Strengths.** A clear, well-articulated product problem (tactical logistics between SASPF/LOGFAS and the battlefield) in a market with strong tailwinds (European defence budgets, NATO LOGFAS modules near end of life). A respected investor (Project A, with a defence practice) and a public integration partnership with Thales whose HexaForce platform is being marketed to NATO countries. Early-team equity upside if the company scales.

**Hard blockers:**
1. **Profitability and maturity:** a pre-seed company about 20 months old, with roughly 5-10 people and no published revenue. LinkedIn shows 2-10 employees (flat at 4-5 for twelve months) and Project A lists it at pre-seed with ~10 people.
2. **No Munich office:** the only premises are a c/o address in the investor's Berlin building. Any office-based role would mean Berlin, Paris or London.
3. **No review or salary data anywhere:** leadership, WLB and pay cannot be checked independently, and there is no works council or collective agreement to compensate.

**Secondary concerns:**
- Defence regulation: security clearance, classified data handling, export control, accreditation overhead and on-prem release cycles.
- Language: Bundeswehr-facing work is in German.
- Single founder/managing director with no visible technical leader; who sets engineering direction is unknown.
- Funding runway: the base-case model points to a seed round being needed by mid-2027; any hiring push depends on that round.
- Tech stack is completely unknown.

**What would change the verdict** (to ask before or during a first call):
1. Current headcount, engineering headcount, and who leads engineering.
2. Last funding round (date, amount, investors) and runway at planned headcount. Current revenue and named customers or contracts.
3. Munich office: address, opening date, how many engineers will sit there, and whether Munich employees are hired by the German GmbH.
4. Salary band and VSOP terms; security clearance requirement; German-language expectations; field/exercise support duties; primary language and stack.

If these come back as a funded seed-stage company with a real Munich office, an engineering lead and a competitive band, it moves to "Monitor". With the public record as it stands, it fails the profitability, size and location criteria.
