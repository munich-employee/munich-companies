# T-Mobile - Full Research Report

_Researched: 2026-11-18_

> **Critical Disqualifiers**
> 1. **No German entity and no Munich office.** T-Mobile US, Inc. states in its FY2025 10-K: "Substantially all of our employees are located throughout the United States, including Puerto Rico." Its postings require being "Legally authorized to work in the United States". In Germany, the old T-Mobile Deutschland GmbH was renamed **Telekom Deutschland GmbH** (Amtsgericht Bonn HRB 5919) and belongs to the parent, Deutsche Telekom AG. T-Mobile International AG and T-Mobile GmbH (Bonn) are dissolved. You cannot be employed by T-Mobile in Munich.
> 2. **Not software-centric.** T-Mobile US is a wireless carrier with 75,000 employees across network, retail, administrative and customer-support roles. Software and IT are internal cost centres, and a current engineer on Blind describes a "heavy contractor culture".
> 3. **Continuous layoffs.** US headcount fell by 4,671 in the first half of 2026 (Deutsche Telekom disclosure). The company recognised $531M of severance for its "2025-2026 Workforce Transformation". This follows about 5,000 corporate and technology cuts in August 2023. Reviewers on Glassdoor and Blind describe a "layoff culture" and "never-ending" layoffs.
> 4. **Weak review scores.** Glassdoor gives 3.6 overall, 3.4 for WLB and 3.0 for senior management, with 42% CEO approval and 47% positive business outlook. Senior Software Engineers recommend it at only 47%. Blind gives 3.4, with Management 2.8 and Career Growth 2.9.
>
> The full report follows for completeness. The only path to a Munich role within this group is the parent, **Deutsche Telekom** (Telekom Deutschland, Deutsche Telekom Technik, T-Systems). That is a different employer and needs its own report. See Section 13.

### Key People

- **Srini Gopalan**: President and CEO since November 2025, previously COO, and before that a Deutsche Telekom board member. Several 2026 Blind reviewers date a culture decline to his arrival ("Good culture before the new CEO taking over").
- **Timotheus Höttges**: Chairman of the Board, and CEO of Deutsche Telekom AG.
- **Headquarters:** Bellevue, Washington and Overland Park, Kansas (10-K).

### Software-Centric Assessment

T-Mobile makes its money from postpaid and prepaid wireless, 5G fixed-wireless home internet and equipment sales. Software engineering serves this business: the T-Life app and digital channels, billing, the customer-care AI stack (IntentCX with OpenAI), network automation and enterprise IT. Engineers are a small minority of the 75,000 staff. Several reviewers describe the IT organisation as non-technical and contractor-heavy:

- "heavy contractor culture, likely half of the company are Accenture contractors" (Blind, Sep 2026, current Software Engineer)
- "A lot of Sr.managers in IT are non-technical." (Blind, Aug 2026, current Software Engineer)
- "not an it company to be honest" (Blind, Oct 2026, current Software Engineer II)

**Verdict: FAIL.** It is a telco with an internal IT function, not a software product company.

## Section 13: Verdict

**Exclude.** You cannot be employed by T-Mobile in Munich, and even in the US the employer would fail several criteria.

**Strengths.** T-Mobile US is very profitable and growing ($11.0B net income, raised 2026 guidance). Day-to-day WLB on most engineering teams is decent (Blind 3.8, Glassdoor SWE 3.7). It has real applied-AI work (IntentCX with OpenAI, agentic customer-care systems), stable US benefits and modest stock grants.

**Hard blockers:**

1. **No German entity and no Munich office.** Per its 10-K, substantially all of the company's 75,000 employees are in the US, and postings require US work authorisation. The only German company that ever carried the name, T-Mobile Deutschland GmbH, is now Telekom Deutschland GmbH (HRB 5919, Bonn), owned by Deutsche Telekom AG.
2. **Not software-centric.** It is a wireless carrier whose engineering is an internal IT function, which reviewers describe as contractor-heavy and run by non-technical managers.
3. **Unstable workforce.** Headcount fell by 4,671 in H1 2026, $531M of severance was booked for the 2025-2026 Workforce Transformation, about 5,000 jobs were cut in 2023, and WARN notices continue through August 2026. Engineers and other staff describe a "layoff culture", with no works council or collective agreement to temper it.
4. **Review scores below threshold.** Glassdoor gives 3.6 overall, 3.4 WLB and 3.0 senior management, with CEO approval of 42% (31% among Senior SWEs) and a 47% recommend rate among Senior Software Engineers. Blind management is 2.8.

**Secondary concerns.** 3-day RTO in the US. On-call on SRE/platform teams. FCC/CPNI regulatory and security overhead. Frequent reorgs and director churn. No promotion paths ("You must apply for every new position"). An AI push that engineers describe as top-down and unvalidated. Interviews often end in ghosting.

**Recommendation.** Do not pursue T-Mobile; no further research is warranted, because the company has no Munich presence. The relevant employer in this group is the parent **Deutsche Telekom AG** and its German subsidiaries: Telekom Deutschland, Deutsche Telekom Technik (Marsplatz 4, München) and T-Systems (Innovation Center at Marsplatz since February 2026). Deutsche Telekom AG has 4.0 on Kununu with 79% recommendation and Top Company status, and as a German group it has works councils and collective agreements. It warrants a **separate** full report (suggested slug: `DeutscheTelekom`), with particular attention to German-language requirements, the T-Systems consulting/outsourcing profile, and Telekom's own German restructuring programmes.
